Manhattan events often land in spaces that were not built as ballrooms: a converted loft south of 23rd Street, a Chelsea gallery, a Midtown office floor after business hours, a Financial District boardroom, a restaurant buyout, or a brownstone parlor on the Upper East or West Side. Tables and chairs for tight Manhattan rooms

Floor space is the first constraint in most Manhattan venues. A standing opening in a loft or gallery usually starts with 24″ or 30″ cocktail tables and a few barstools along the wall, leaving a clear path through the middle of the room. Seated dinners in hotel event rooms and townhouse parlors more often use 60″ rounds, which seat 8–10, or 48″ rounds when the room is narrow. Chiavari chairs are a frequent pair for those tables; folding chairs are a practical choice for talks, screenings and overflow seating along a wall. Bars that fit loft corners and reception rooms

Many Manhattan lofts and galleries have no built-in bar, and a Midtown conference floor rarely does either. A rented bar, placed against a column or in an alcove, gives bartenders a defined station without blocking the main circulation. Choose from bar rental or the bars catalog, then add glassware and barware to match the menu. For a cocktail-heavy opening, plan pickup space at both ends of a longer bar so a line does not cut the room in half.

Lounge furniture for foyers and VIP corners

Pre-function corridors, hotel foyers and the far end of a loft benefit from a small lounge grouping: a sofa or loveseat, a coffee table and a rug, sometimes with a chaise. Guests use these pockets during remarks, while a presentation runs in the next room, or as a quieter alternative to cocktail tables. See event furniture and lounge rentals and lounge furniture. Measure the pocket you want to fill, including radiator and column clearances, and include those notes with the quote.

Linens for hotel rooms, lofts and townhouses

A hotel event floor, a loft with north light and a wood-floored townhouse dining room each ask for a different tabletop finish. Linen rentals cover cloths and overlays that we quote with the tables and chairs. Send a photo of the room or a color cue from the invitation and we will help you pick a finish that holds up in that light.

Gallery walls in Chelsea and across Manhattan

Galleries, pop-ups and brand exhibits use our wall panels to build temporary hanging surface without touching the building's walls. Every panel is double-sided, 4 feet wide by 7 feet tall; five panels make a 20-foot run, which is a common length along a Chelsea gallery's long axis. Start with Chelsea gallery walls or Manhattan gallery walls, or see the full gallery wall panels and portable gallery walls pages.

Freight elevators, service entrances and street access

High-rise load-ins in Midtown and the Financial District almost always run through a booked freight elevator and a service entrance, often on a tight window. Brownstones and smaller walk-ups may mean stairs and a stoop, with little or no standing time at the curb. Events near Central Park, Bryant Park or the High Line can add extra walking distance from the nearest legal unloading spot. Include the floor number, elevator hours, dock rules, and any park-path or curb notes when you request a quote so delivery and pickup can be planned around the building, not against it.
